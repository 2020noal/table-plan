# Table Plan — Video Demo Script

A ~3-minute walkthrough that takes a viewer from a messy guest-list export to a
finished, printable seating chart. Written to be read aloud while you screen-record.

**Before you hit record**
- Open `seating-chart-planner.html` in Chrome, full-screen, clean browser (no bookmarks bar, no other tabs).
- Have `sample-guest-list.csv` on your desktop, in view.
- Zoom the browser to ~110% so text is legible in the recording.
- If you've used the tool before, open a fresh copy (or click "Start with an empty chart" and refresh) so there's no leftover autosave prompt.
- Turn on Do Not Disturb so notifications don't appear.

Timings are targets, not rules. The voiceover (VO) is written to be spoken at a
relaxed pace. Bracketed lines are on-screen actions.

---

## 0:00 — Hook (cold open, ~12s)

**[On screen: the messy CSV open in a spreadsheet — 87 rows, RSVP column with Attending / Declined / No Response mixed in.]**

**VO:** "This is what a real guest list looks like. Eighty-seven names, some declined, some haven't replied, families scattered across rows. Turning that into a seating chart usually means an afternoon with a spreadsheet and a pile of sticky notes."

**[Cut to the app's clean start screen.]**

**VO:** "Here's the whole thing in about two minutes. No account, no upload — it all runs in one file on your computer."

---

## 0:12 — Import (~25s)

**[Drag `sample-guest-list.csv` onto the drop zone.]**

**VO:** "Drag your export straight in. Works with a CSV from any RSVP site — Zola, The Knot, a Google Sheet, whatever you've got. It can also read a printed PDF list."

**[The Columns step appears with the preview table.]**

**VO:** "It reads the file and figures out the columns on its own — first name, last name, title. And notice this: it found the RSVP column and set it to 'Attending' automatically."

**[Point cursor at the filter row: RSVP = Attending. Point at the count line.]**

**VO:** "So the twelve people who declined or haven't responded are already filtered out. Seventy-five guests coming through. If it ever guesses wrong, every column is a dropdown you can change."

**[Click "Review guests →".]**

---

## 0:37 — Review (~20s)

**[The review list is on screen, 75 checked names with titles.]**

**VO:** "Now you get a clean pass over the list. Everyone's here with their proper title — Mr., Mrs., Dr., Rabbi. Uncheck anyone who isn't coming."

**[Untick one name to show it works. Then click a name and fix a typo inline.]**

**VO:** "Click a name to fix a typo. Add anyone the file missed down here."

**[Type a name into "Add a name," e.g. "Ms. Dana Ellis," click Add.]**

**VO:** "And if you spot duplicates from a double-invite, one button clears them."

**[Click "Set up the room →".]**

---

## 0:57 — Room setup (~18s)

**[The room step shows Round tables suggested, capacity bar filling.]**

**VO:** "Tell it roughly what your room looks like. It's already suggested enough round tables for seventy-five guests — see the capacity bar? Green means everyone fits."

**[Change a number — bump round tables, or add a banquet row. Type an event name.]**

**VO:** "Add a long banquet table, change the counts, whatever matches your venue. Give it a name…"

**[Type "Rachel & David" in Event name. Click "Open the plan →".]**

**VO:** "…and open the plan."

---

## 1:15 — The plan + auto-fill (~22s)

**[The floor plan is on screen: empty tables, guest list in the sidebar.]**

**VO:** "Here's your room. Every guest is in the sidebar, sorted by last name. You can drag names onto seats one by one — but let's not do that seventy-five times."

**[Click "Auto-fill" in the top bar. Tables populate.]**

**VO:** "Auto-fill seats everyone in seconds, and — this matters — it keeps households together. The Patels came in as a family, and they're all at the same table. Same for every couple and family in the list."

**[Hover a table to show a family clustered together.]**

---

## 1:37 — Couples (~25s)

**[Click a guest in the sidebar to open their menu; choose "Link as a couple…"]**

**VO:** "You can also mark two people as a couple. Say these two are dating but aren't in the same household on the list —"

**[Search the partner's name in the picker, click to link. A heart appears on both.]**

**VO:** "— link them, and now they travel together. Watch: if I seat one…"

**[Drag one of the linked pair onto a table with an open seat.]**

**VO:** "…their partner is seated right at the same table automatically. And if a couple ever ends up apart, the app flags it down here in red so you catch it before it's printed."

**[Point at the "♥ Couples" toggle in the top bar.]**

**VO:** "It's optional — flip this off any time and you can seat people wherever you like."

---

## 2:02 — Editing the room (~20s)

**[Click a seat with a guest; show the seat menu — move, remove chair, etc.]**

**VO:** "Everything's editable. Click a seat to move someone, swap two guests, or take a chair out entirely."

**[Click a table's center to open the table menu. Add a seat, rotate, rename.]**

**VO:** "Click a table to add or remove seats, rotate it, rename it, or drag the whole thing to match your real floor plan."

**[Drag a table across the canvas. Then press Ctrl+Z.]**

**VO:** "Made a mistake? Ctrl-Z undoes anything — even auto-fill."

---

## 2:22 — Export (~25s)

**[Click the "By table" tab.]**

**VO:** "When it's done, switch to the list views. Here's every table with its guests."

**[Click "Download PDF" on the By table view. The print-ready page opens.]**

**VO:** "Export a clean PDF for the caterer or the venue — proper typography, one tidy document."

**[Switch to "By name," click its Download PDF too, show the directory look briefly.]**

**VO:** "Or a guest directory sorted by name, so anyone can find their table at a glance. There's a CSV export on both too, if you'd rather have a spreadsheet."

**[Back on the plan, show the Export menu → place cards.]**

**VO:** "It'll even print place cards for every seat."

---

## 2:47 — Save + close (~18s)

**[Click "Save chart" in the top bar. The file downloads.]**

**VO:** "One last thing — saving. Click Save, and it downloads a copy of the file with your entire chart baked in."

**[Open the downloaded file in a new tab; it goes straight to the finished plan.]**

**VO:** "Open that file any time and you're right back where you left off. No cloud, no login, nothing to lose. It's yours."

**[Cut to a clean end card: product name + where to buy.]**

**VO:** "That's the whole thing. Link below."

## One-line captions (for a silent / social version)

1. Drop in your guest list — CSV or PDF
2. It filters out declines and reads your columns automatically
3. Review and fix names in one pass
4. Auto-fill seats everyone — families stay together
5. Mark couples so they never get split up
6. Drag, swap, and edit every table
7. Export a beautiful PDF or CSV — even place cards
8. Save the file and open it anytime. No account. No upload.

## Notes / gotchas while recording

- **Auto-fill is random-ish per run.** If you want the Patel-family beat to land, do a dry run first and confirm they cluster; re-run auto-fill (undo, then auto-fill again) until it reads well on screen. Or seat that one family by hand right before, off-camera.
- **The couples "auto-follow" beat** is the strongest moment — rehearse it so the partner visibly pops into the same table. Pick a couple where the target table clearly has an open seat.
- **PDF export opens the browser print dialog.** In the recording, let it open, hover "Save as PDF," and cut before the OS save sheet — or narrate "choose Save as PDF." Don't leave the raw print dialog on screen too long.
- **Fonts:** the PDF looks best on a machine with internet (it pulls the Fraunces display font). Record on a connected machine so the exported PDF shows the real typography.
- **Reset between takes:** to get the clean import flow again, refresh the page. If an autosave "pick up where you left off" box appears, click "Start fresh."
